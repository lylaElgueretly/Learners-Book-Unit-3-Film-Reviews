Reviewing films: finding and writing evaluative sentences

Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary English, Unit 3 – Film and Fame

Lesson objective

To identify evaluative sentences in a film review, and to write sentences that contrast a film's strengths and weaknesses using connectives such as although, however, despite and whereas.

Starter (5 minutes)

What makes a film good or bad? For each point below, decide: is it something a reviewer could say a film does well or badly, or is it just a fixed fact that can't really be judged? Tap your answer for each, then reveal the summary.

The extract

Car Trouble: a film with no fuel in the tank

By Ali Ling

Activity 1: Spot the evaluative sentences

An evaluative sentence gives a judgement about the film – it says something is good, bad, disappointing, exciting and so on, rather than just describing what happens. Click on every sentence in the extract above that you think is evaluative.

Hint: look for words that judge quality (best, exciting, disappoint, shame, excellent) rather than words that simply report events (crash, kidnapped, catches up).

Activity 2: Write contrasting sentences

Good reviewers often put a strength and a weakness in the same sentence, joined by a contrast connective (although, however, despite, whereas, while, but). Use each pair of ideas below to write one contrasting sentence of your own.

Sentence 1
+ The car chase is thrilling and well filmed.
− The family scenes in the second half are dull.
Hint: start with a connective, e.g. "Although..." or "While...", then give the positive point, then the negative point.
Model answer: Although the car chase is thrilling and well filmed, the family scenes in the second half are dull.
Sentence 2
+ Jonny Jonsson is a talented actor.
− His talent is wasted in this disappointing film.
Hint: try "Despite..." followed by a noun phrase (Despite being a talented actor...), then the negative point.
Model answer: Despite being a talented actor, Jonny Jonsson is wasted in this disappointing film.
Sentence 3
+ The film's acting is strong throughout.
− Very little actually happens in the plot.
Hint: this is very close to a sentence actually used in the review itself – look at the last line of the second paragraph for inspiration.
Model answer (from the original review): Although it's well acted, nothing much happens.
